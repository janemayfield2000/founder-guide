Capterra, GetApp, G2, and Software Advice: How Software Directories Generate B2B Leads

If you sell software, your buyers are almost certainly using software directories before they talk to you. They search for a category, scan a shortlist, read what other users say, and only then visit vendor websites. A company that is missing from those shortlists is missing from the conversation.

Four platforms come up again and again in this space: Capterra, GetApp, G2, and Software Advice. Each works a little differently, and knowing those differences helps you decide where to invest your time first.

Why Software Buyers Start on Directories

Software purchases are rarely impulsive. A buyer usually has a problem, a rough budget, and a long list of possible solutions. Directories compress that research into one place by showing descriptions, pricing, key features, screenshots, and user reviews side by side.

For a vendor, that means a directory listing is often the first real impression a prospect has. Buyers who arrive through these platforms are motivated and already comparing options, which makes them different from casual visitors who land on your blog.

What Each Platform Brings

Each of the major names has its own strengths:

  • Capterra and GetApp. Capterra is reported at more than five million monthly visitors and supports lead capture through product inquiries and RFP submissions. GetApp, owned by Gartner, is reported to send over 750,000 software buyers to vendor sites each month, which gives it weight among IT decision-makers.
  • G2 and Software Advice. G2 is built around user reviews, ratings, and detailed product comparisons, with more than one million monthly visitors reported. Software Advice works as a recommendation engine, with 200,000+ monthly visitors, and can bring targeted leads from corporate buyers.

The guide to top business directory websites for B2B companies from ListingBott covers these platforms in more detail and is a useful companion when building your shortlist.

Matching a Platform to Your Goals

Not every directory fits every product. If your priority is capturing inquiries, a platform that supports product inquiries and RFP submissions may serve you best. If your priority is credibility in a crowded market, a review-driven platform where buyers compare products directly can do more for you.

Think about where your own customers would search. A tool for IT teams may find its audience on GetApp, while a product that depends on strong user feedback may gain more from a review-focused site. Start with the one or two platforms that match your buyer, then expand.

Building a Profile That Converts

A listing is not a brochure. It is a decision aid, and it should answer the questions buyers are already asking: what does this product do, who is it for, what does it cost, and how does it connect to the tools I already use.

Fill in every available field, including pricing details, target industries, integrations, and partnerships. Write a description that leads with the value you deliver rather than a list of features, and use the keywords your audience actually types. Add screenshots and demo videos so visitors can see the product instead of imagining it.

Using Reviews on Software Platforms

On software directories, reviews often carry as much weight as your own description. Ask satisfied customers to share feedback soon after a success, such as a smooth onboarding or a solved problem. Make it easy by sending a direct link and keeping the request short.

Respond to every review professionally. Thank positive reviewers with something specific, and answer critical ones calmly with a path to resolution. Prospects reading the thread often care more about how you respond than about the complaint itself.

Keeping Your Presence Current

Software changes quickly, and your listings should keep pace. Update your profiles when you release new features, add integrations, or announce partnerships. An outdated profile can make a healthy product look neglected.

Review each listing on a regular schedule to confirm that pricing, descriptions, and screenshots still match your current product. A short quarterly check is usually enough to catch the details that drift.

Tracking What Works

Treat directory marketing as a channel you can measure. Watch where referral traffic comes from, which listings produce inquiries, and which platforms bring visitors who go on to convert. Those patterns tell you where to spend more time and where to scale back.

You do not need to be everywhere. A focused presence on the platforms that send qualified buyers will outperform a thin presence on every site you can find.

The Bottom Line

Software directories give vendors something hard to buy elsewhere: placement in front of buyers who are already evaluating solutions. They support visibility, credibility, and lead generation at the same time, though they work best as part of a broader marketing mix rather than a standalone strategy.

Choose the platforms that match your buyer, complete every field, collect reviews steadily, and keep your listings fresh. Done consistently, that routine turns directory profiles into a dependable source of qualified leads.
