Alternatives Sites and Software Catalogs: How HR Vendors Can Use Them Without Overestimating Them

When vendors plan their HR software visibility, the big review platforms get most of the attention. Software catalogs and alternatives sites usually land at the bottom of the list, if they make it at all. That is not entirely fair. These surfaces do a specific job, and used with realistic expectations, they can fill gaps that larger platforms leave open.

What Catalogs and Alternatives Sites Actually Do

Catalog-style platforms such as SaaSHub, Serchen, and FinancesOnline add extra software-discovery surface area. Alternatives platforms such as AlternativeTo serve a narrower purpose: they catch buyers who are already using a tool and are looking for a replacement.

That distinction matters. A review site helps a buyer compare vendors in a category. An alternatives site helps a buyer who has already decided to leave something behind. The intent is different, and so is the kind of profile that works. For a broader look at how these surfaces fit alongside the rest of the market, this guide to HR software directories and review sites is a helpful companion.

Where They Fit in the Buyer’s Journey

These platforms are most useful in two moments. The first is early discovery, when buyers are still learning what kind of tool they need and are reading broadly across the category. SaaSHub and FinancesOnline can help people enter the category and see where a product sits within it.

The second is emerging-tool discovery, which matters most for newer HR products and category challengers. A young vendor rarely has the review volume to compete on the biggest platforms right away. Catalogs and alternatives sites give it additional places to be found while that review base grows.

The Honest Limits

It is important to be clear about what these sites do not do. Buyer intent on catalog and alternatives platforms is generally weaker than on major review sites. Editorial-style pages can vary widely in practical depth, and a few of these sites are better suited to supplemental visibility than to closing an evaluation.

AlternativeTo, for example, is useful when buyers are actively comparing replacements, but it is not an ideal primary channel for HR discovery. Serchen can add another relevant software-category surface, but for most HR vendors it remains a secondary priority. Treating these sites as the foundation of a strategy usually leads to disappointment.

How to Prepare a Profile That Works Here

A lighter platform does not excuse a lighter profile. Catalog and alternatives listings still benefit from the same basics: one clear primary category, a short positioning line, and a description that explains the problem your product solves.

For alternatives sites in particular, it helps to think about the switcher. Who is leaving another tool, and why? A profile that speaks to that person, with specifics about team type, use case, and what makes the transition manageable, is far more useful than a generic overview. Keep your naming consistent with the rest of your presence so buyers and search engines see the same product everywhere.

Where They Belong in Your Plan

The sensible order is to secure the core first. Build strong profiles on the major review platforms and the niche HR directories, then add catalog and alternatives sites as the second layer. Doing it in reverse tends to produce plenty of listings and very little shortlist traffic.

Think of this layer as reinforcement. It will not carry your visibility alone, but it expands your footprint in the places buyers wander through while they research, and that has real value once the foundation is in place.

The Bottom Line

Software catalogs and alternatives sites are neither essential nor worthless. They are a supporting layer that works best after your core presence is built. Give them a clear role, write for the buyer who finds you there, and keep your expectations grounded.
